## SOP-FM-01: Daily Facilities Inspection

* **SOP Code:** SOP-FM-01
* **SOP Title:** Daily Facilities Inspection
* **Department:** Facilities & Engineering
* **Effective Date:** October 2026
* **Revision Number:** 1.0

### 1. Objective & Scope
To perform systematic daily morning walkthrough inspections across all physical structures, guest spaces, utility rooms, and public grounds to identify maintenance deficiencies, safety hazards, or equipment malfunctions before they impact guests.

### 2. Responsible Staff Roles
* **Maintenance Supervisor:** Conducts primary morning inspection walkthrough and assigns work tickets.
* **Duty Maintenance Technician:** Assists in testing mechanical and electrical fixtures during the walkthrough.
* **General Manager:** Performs random spot checks and reviews daily inspection logs.

### 3. Step-by-Step Execution Procedure
1. **Initiate Morning Walkthrough (07:00 AM):** Retrieve the **Daily Facilities Walkthrough Checklist** and master keys from the Engineering Office.
2. **Public Areas & Lobby Inspection:**
   * Inspect all interior and exterior lighting fixtures for burnt-out bulbs or loose wiring.
   * Check lobby air conditioning operation, target setpoint at $22^\circ\text{C} - 24^\circ\text{C}$.
   * Verify doors, glass panels, locks, and automatic closures operate smoothly.
3. **Walkway & Grounds Inspection:**
   * Check pathway pavers, perimeter walls, and outdoor lamps for cracks, trip hazards, or weather damage.
   * Verify clear drainage pathways along garden perimeters.
4. **Utility & Plant Room Inspection:**
   * Inspect main circuit breaker panels for excessive heat, buzzing sounds, or tripped breakers.
   * Record water supply pressure gauge readings ($40 - 60\text{ psi}$).
   * Inspect transfer pump and booster pump for leaks, abnormal vibration, or overheating.
   * Check backup diesel generator fuel gauge ($\ge 80\%$ full) and battery voltage meter ($12\text{V} / 24\text{V}$).
5. **Log & Action Assignment:** Log all identified defects into the digital PMS Maintenance Module or Physical Engineering Logbook. Issue immediate work tickets for high-priority items.

### 4. Localized Context
* **Salt Air Spray & Corrosion:** Gamay’s coastal atmosphere accelerates rust on outdoor metal lamps, door hinges, and screws. Inspect exterior metal fixtures daily for salt crusting and early corrosion.
* **Heavy Rainfall Accumulation:** During the Samar rainy season, check garden drainage basins daily to prevent localized pooling near ground-floor guest rooms.

### 5. Quality Assurance Checklist & Metrics
* [ ] Walkthrough completed and logged prior to 08:30 AM daily.
* [ ] Water supply pressure verified between $40\text{ psi}$ and $60\text{ psi}$.
* [ ] Generator fuel level verified at $\ge 80\%$.
* **Target Metric:** $100\%$ daily inspection completion rate; zero unaddressed high-priority hazards past 09:00 AM.

## SOP-FM-02: Preventive Maintenance Program

* **SOP Code:** SOP-FM-02
* **SOP Title:** Preventive Maintenance Program
* **Department:** Facilities & Engineering
* **Effective Date:** October 2026
* **Revision Number:** 1.0

### 1. Objective & Scope
To maintain a scheduled, proactive maintenance calendar for all guest rooms, mechanical equipment, HVAC systems, and building structures to extend asset life and minimize unexpected operational breakdowns.

### 2. Responsible Staff Roles
* **Chief Engineer / Maintenance Supervisor:** Manages the Master PM Schedule, orders spare parts, and oversees execution.
* **Maintenance Technicians:** Executes scheduled PM tasks according to equipment checklists.
* **Front Office Manager:** Coordinates room blocks for Room Preventive Maintenance (RPM) cycles.

### 3. Step-by-Step Execution Procedure
1. **Master PM Calendar Maintenance:** Establish annual, quarterly, monthly, and weekly preventive maintenance schedules covering:
   * **Guest Rooms (RPM):** Cycle through all 30 rooms ($1\text{ room per day}$, completing a full cycle every month).
   * **HVAC Systems:** Deep coil cleaning and filter washing quarterly.
   * **Generators & Pumps:** Engine oil, filter, and belt replacements every $250\text{ run hours}$ or semi-annually.
   * **Pool Filtration:** Backwashing sand filters weekly; deep pump strainer cleanouts bi-weekly.
2. **Room PM (RPM) Execution:**
   * Block room with Front Office as "Out of Service - Maintenance" for 4 hours.
   * Test AC cooling performance, flush drain line, clean filters.
   * Inspect all plumbing fixtures, water heater tank, flush valve, and drain flow rate.
   * Inspect electrical outlets, light switches, TV/cable connection, and safe box operation.
   * Check furniture stability, silicon sealants around shower doors, and door lock battery voltage.
3. **Equipment PM Execution:** Follow manufacturer service manuals for all heavy equipment (pumps, generator, kitchen freezers, water heaters).
4. **Sign-Off & Logging:** Record completed PM tasks in the Master Asset Card and PMS PM Log.

### 4. Localized Context
* **High Humidity & Mold Mitigation:** In Samar’s tropical climate, AC condensate lines accumulate mold rapidly. Flush all room AC drain lines with a mild bleach solution during monthly RPM to prevent indoor water leaks.
* **Pre-Typhoon PM Surge:** Schedule comprehensive PM checks on all roof flashings, gutters, and generator systems prior to the peak typhoon season (September to December).

### 5. Quality Assurance Checklist & Metrics
* [ ] All 30 guest rooms undergo complete RPM at least once every $30\text{ days}$.
* [ ] Generator maintenance log updated after every service interval.
* [ ] AC cooling temperature delta verified ($\ge 8^\circ\text{C}$ difference between intake and supply air).
* **Target Metric:** $\ge 95\%$ adherence to Master PM Schedule; $< 2\%$ emergency breakdown rate on PM-covered assets.

## SOP-FM-03: Corrective Maintenance & Work Orders

* **SOP Code:** SOP-FM-03
* **SOP Title:** Corrective Maintenance & Work Orders
* **Department:** Facilities & Engineering
* **Effective Date:** October 2026
* **Revision Number:** 1.0

### 1. Objective & Scope
To provide a fast, standardized workflow for reporting, prioritizing, assigning, executing, and closing corrective maintenance work orders across all departments.

### 2. Responsible Staff Roles
* **Department Heads / Staff (Initiators):** Spot defects and submit Work Orders via PMS or physical work order slips.
* **Maintenance Supervisor:** Receives, prioritizes, and assigns work tickets to technicians.
* **Duty Maintenance Technician:** Executes repairs and reports completion details.

### 3. Step-by-Step Execution Procedure
1. **Work Order Creation:** Upon discovering a defect (e.g., leaking faucet, dead light, noisy AC), the observing staff member creates a Work Order ticket specifying:
   * Location / Room Number
   * Specific Description of Problem
   * Date and Time Reported
   * Priority Level: **Urgent** (Life safety/Guest discomfort), **High** (Operational impairment), **Normal** (Cosmetic/Routine).
2. **Priority Triaging:**
   * **Urgent (Response $< 15\text{ mins}$):** Power failure, active plumbing leak, AC failure in occupied room, door lock failure.
   * **High (Response $< 2\text{ hours}$):** Public restroom defect, kitchen equipment malfunction, pool light failure.
   * **Normal (Response $< 24\text{ hours}$):** Loose cabinet hinge, minor touch-up paint, garden fixture repair.
3. **Dispatch & Repair:** Technician retrieves ticket, gathers necessary tools/parts from storage, and proceeds to location.
4. **In-Room Repair Protocol:**
   * Knock 3 times, announcing "Maintenance Service."
   * If occupied, politely explain reason for visit and estimated duration.
   * Place drop cloth under work zone to protect resort carpet or tile floor.
   * Complete repair cleanly and sanitize work area post-repair.
5. **Testing & Sign-Off:** Test repaired equipment in presence of requesting department lead or guest. Obtain requesting department signature on Work Order.

### 4. Localized Context
* **Provincial Parts Sourcing Delays:** If replacement parts are not in stock locally in Gamay and require procurement from Catarman or Tacloban ($2 - 5\text{ days}$ lead time), immediately notify Front Office to keep the guest room out of order or offer room change.

### 5. Quality Assurance Checklist & Metrics
* [ ] Urgent work orders responded to within $15\text{ minutes}$.
* [ ] Repair site left completely clean and sanitized.
* [ ] Requesting department signature obtained upon completion.
* **Target Metric:** $90\%$ of work orders resolved within target response time; $0$ unaddressed guest-room tickets past $24\text{ hours}$.

## SOP-FM-04: Electrical System Inspection

* **SOP Code:** SOP-FM-04
* **SOP Title:** Electrical System Inspection
* **Department:** Facilities & Engineering
* **Effective Date:** October 2026
* **Revision Number:** 1.0

### 1. Objective & Scope
To ensure safe, continuous electrical power distribution, prevent electrical fires, monitor voltage stability, and maintain all primary and secondary distribution panels across the property.

### 2. Responsible Staff Roles
* **Licensed Electrician / Maintenance Tech:** Performs electrical panel inspections, thermal testing, and load balancing.
* **Chief Engineer:** Reviews electrical inspection logs and oversees high-voltage maintenance.

### 3. Step-by-Step Execution Procedure
1. **Daily Main Breaker Panel Inspection:**
   * Inspect Main Distribution Panel (MDP) and Sub-Distribution Panels (SDPs).
   * Measure incoming supply line voltage ($220\text{V} \pm 10\%$, $60\text{ Hz}$).
   * Check panel door seals to prevent moisture, dust, or insect entry.
2. **Monthly Thermal Scan & Wire Tightening:**
   * De-energize sub-panels during scheduled low-occupancy window using proper Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) safety protocol.
   * Check for loose terminal screws caused by thermal expansion; torque to manufacturer specification.
   * Use an infrared thermal scanner to detect overheating breakers or wiring busbars (temperature $> 60^\circ\text{C}$ indicates overload or loose connection).
3. **Grounding System Verification:**
   * Inspect main ground rod connections quarterly.
   * Verify Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter (GFCI) outlets in bathrooms, kitchen wet zones, and swimming pool perimeter function correctly by pressing the "Test/Reset" button monthly.
4. **Emergency Lighting & Battery Bank Check:** Test all emergency hallway exit signs and battery backup emergency lights monthly for a minimum $30\text{-minute}$ illumination run.

### 4. Localized Context
* **NORSAMELCO Grid Voltage Fluctuations:** The local electrical grid in Northern Samar experiences frequent voltage surges and brownouts. **MANDATORY:** Verify that main Automatic Voltage Regulators (AVRs) and surge protectors servicing sensitive hotel equipment (PMS server, elevators, kitchen refrigeration, AV gear) are operational daily.

### 5. Quality Assurance Checklist & Metrics
* [ ] Line voltage verified daily within $198\text{V} - 242\text{V}$ range.
* [ ] All GFCI wet-zone outlets tested monthly.
* [ ] Electrical panel thermal scan completed quarterly.
* **Target Metric:** $0$ electrical fire incidents; $100\%$ functional GFCI outlet protection in wet zones.

* **Department:** Facilities & Engineering
* **Effective Date:** October 2026
* **Revision Number:** 1.0

To maintain leak-free, clean, and efficient fresh water supply lines, water heaters, sanitary drainage, septic tanks, and kitchen grease traps throughout the resort.

### 2. Responsible Staff Roles
* **Duty Plumber / Maintenance Tech:** Conducts daily plumbing checks, unblocks lines, and services water heaters.
* **Executive Housekeeper:** Reports room water leaks or slow drainage immediately to Engineering.

### 3. Step-by-Step Execution Procedure
1. **Daily Water Pressure Check:** Check manifold pressure gauges at booster pump output; maintain between $40\text{ psi}$ and $60\text{ psi}$ to ensure strong shower pressure across all 30 rooms.
2. **Weekly Guest Room Plumbing Audit:**
   * Inspect under-sink angle valves, flex hoses, and p-traps for moisture or slow drips.
   * Test shower mixer valve, verify hot water delivery reaches $45^\circ\text{C} - 50^\circ\text{C}$ within $45\text{ seconds}$.

3. **Sanitary Sewer & Septic System Maintenance:**
   * Inspect septic tank levels monthly. Schedule desludging via certified local waste contractor when solids reach $2/3$ capacity.

4. **Drain Line Unblocking:** Use drain snake or plunger for clogs. Avoid harsh chemical drain cleaners that damage PVC piping and destroy septic tank anaerobic bacterial cultures.

### 4. Localized Context
* **Hard Water Mineral Scaling:** Local deep well water sources in Northern Samar often contain elevated mineral content. Perform bi-monthly descaling of showerheads and water heater heating elements using vinegar or commercial descaling solution to prevent clogging.

### 5. Quality Assurance Checklist & Metrics
* [ ] Hot water temperature delivered at $45^\circ\text{C} - 50^\circ\text{C}$ within $45\text{ seconds}$.
* [ ] Kitchen grease traps cleaned and logged weekly.
* [ ] Zero silent toilet flapper leaks active across 30 rooms.
* **Target Metric:** Water loss from leaks maintained at $< 3\%$ of total utility consumption.

## SOP-FM-06: Air-Conditioning Maintenance

* **SOP Code:** SOP-FM-06
* **SOP Title:** Air-Conditioning Maintenance
* **Department:** Facilities & Engineering
* **Effective Date:** October 2026
* **Revision Number:** 1.0

### 1. Objective & Scope
To maximize cooling efficiency, maintain indoor air quality, optimize power consumption, and prevent water leakage from split-type and window-type air conditioning units across all rooms and public areas.

### 2. Responsible Staff Roles
* **HVAC Technician:** Performs bi-weekly filter cleaning, quarterly chemical coil washing, and refrigerant pressure checks.
* **Maintenance Supervisor:** Schedules preventive service cycles and monitors AC energy performance.

### 3. Step-by-Step Execution Procedure
1. **Bi-Weekly Air Filter Cleaning:**
   * Turn off AC power breaker. Remove air filters from evaporator unit.
   * Wash filters under clean tap water using soft brush; dry completely in shade before reinstalling.
2. **Quarterly Chemical Coil Cleaning:**
   * Place protective waterproof jacket around indoor evaporator unit.
   * Apply biodegradable coil cleaning solution to aluminum fins; flush thoroughly with low-pressure washer.
   * Clear condensate drain tray and flush drain line using pressurized water or bleach mixture.
   * Wash outdoor condenser coil fins to remove dirt, salt crust, and debris.
3. **Refrigerant & Performance Audit:**
   * Measure operating suction pressure ($R410\text{A}$: $120 - 140\text{ psi}$; $R32$: $110 - 130\text{ psi}$).
   * Measure air temperature differential ($\Delta T = T_{\text{return}} - T_{\text{supply}}$). A properly operating unit must yield $\Delta T \ge 8^\circ\text{C}$.
   * Check compressor running amperage against rated nameplate value.

### 4. Localized Context
* **Coastal Anti-Corrosion Care:** Coastal salt spray degrades aluminum condenser fins rapidly. Apply anti-corrosion blue-coat protective spray to all outdoor condenser coils semi-annually to extend coil life in Gamay's marine environment.

### 5. Quality Assurance Checklist & Metrics
* [ ] Air filters cleaned every 14 days.
* [ ] Temperature differential $\Delta T \ge 8^\circ\text{C}$ verified post-service.
* [ ] Condensate drain line flushed during every coil cleaning.
* **Target Metric:** Zero in-room AC condensate leaks; $100\%$ scheduled chemical wash completion rate.

## SOP-FM-08: Generator & Backup Power Procedure

* **SOP Code:** SOP-FM-08
* **SOP Title:** Generator & Backup Power Procedure
* **Department:** Facilities & Engineering
* **Effective Date:** October 2026
* **Revision Number:** 1.0

### 1. Objective & Scope
To operate, test, and maintain the resort’s primary diesel generator set and Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS) to guarantee seamless emergency power restoration within $15\text{ seconds}$ of an electrical grid outage.

### 2. Responsible Staff Roles
* **Duty Maintenance Tech / Generator Operator:** Performs daily checks, fuel management, and manual changeovers if required.
* **Chief Engineer:** Supervises load testing, ATS servicing, and fuel supplier agreements.

### 3. Step-by-Step Execution Procedure
1. **Daily Generator Readiness Inspection:**
   * Check diesel fuel level in main base tank; must remain $\ge 80\%$ full (minimum $24\text{ hours}$ continuous full-load run capacity).
   * Check engine oil level, coolant radiator level, and drive belt tension.
   * Verify starter battery trickler charger voltage ($13.5\text{V} - 14.2\text{V}$).
   * Ensure ATS control panel switch is set to **"AUTO"** mode.
2. **Automatic Outage Sequence Execution:**
   * Upon grid power failure, ATS senses line loss, signals generator engine start within $3\text{ seconds}$, and transfers load within $10 - 15\text{ seconds}$.
   * Duty Tech immediately verifies generator voltage ($220\text{V}$), frequency ($60\text{ Hz}$), and engine water temperature ($80^\circ\text{C} - 90^\circ\text{C}$).
3. **Manual Override Procedure (In Case of ATS Failure):**
   * Switch ATS selector switch from "AUTO" to "MANUAL".
   * Press "START" button on generator control panel; allow engine warm-up for $30\text{ seconds}$.
   * Manually throw ATS main breaker switch to "GENERATOR" position.
4. **Weekly No-Load / Monthly Load Testing:** Run generator under actual resort load for $30\text{ minutes}$ on the 1st and 15th day of every month to keep engine lubricated and purge moist air from alternator windings.

### 4. Localized Context
* **Frequent NORSAMELCO Grid Outages:** Northern Samar experiences scheduled and unscheduled power blackouts. Keep a minimum reserve stock of $500\text{L}$ of clean diesel fuel on-site in approved double-walled storage tanks to handle prolonged regional power outages.

### 5. Quality Assurance Checklist & Metrics
* [ ] Automatic transfer completed within $15\text{ seconds}$ of grid failure.
* [ ] Fuel level maintained at $\ge 80\%$ daily.
* [ ] Monthly load test executed and recorded in Generator Logbook.
* **Target Metric:** $100\%$ backup power reliability during grid outages.

* **SOP Title:** Solar Power & Energy System Monitoring
* **Department:** Facilities & Engineering
* **Effective Date:** October 2026
* **Revision Number:** 1.0

To monitor, clean, and maintain the resort's solar photovoltaic (PV) array and hybrid inverter system to maximize renewable energy output and reduce utility power expenses.

### 2. Responsible Staff Roles

* **Chief Engineer:** Tracks daily power production analytics ($\text{kWh}$) against resort utility benchmarks.

### 3. Step-by-Step Execution Procedure

### 5. Quality Assurance Checklist & Metrics

* [ ] Inverter generation metrics recorded daily.

## SOP-FM-18: Pool Water Testing

* **Effective Date:** October 2026

### 2. Responsible Staff Roles

* **Maintenance Supervisor:** Audits test logs and calculates corrective chemical dosing schedules.

1. **Testing Schedule:** Perform water testing 3 times daily:
   * **Test 1:** 05:30 AM (Pre-opening)
   * **Test 2:** 12:00 PM (Peak sun / High bather load)

2. **Sample Collection Method:**
   * Take water sample at elbow depth ($18\text{ inches} / 45\text{ cm}$ below surface) away from return jets and skimmer openings.
3. **Parameter Ranges & Standard Testing Protocol:**
   * **Free Available Chlorine (FAC):** Ideal range **$1.0 - 3.0\text{ ppm}$** (Use DPD #1 tablet/reagent).
   * **pH Level:** Ideal range **$7.2 - 7.8$** (Use Phenol Red reagent).
   * **Total Alkalinity (TA):** Tested weekly; Ideal range **$80 - 120\text{ ppm}$**.

### 4. Localized Context
* **Tropical Sunlight & Rain Impact:** Intense tropical sun in Northern Samar rapidly degrades free chlorine via UV rays (requiring proper stabilizer/CYA levels). Heavy tropical rains dilute chlorine and lower pH drastically; test water immediately post-downpours.

### 5. Quality Assurance Checklist & Metrics
* [ ] Water tested and logged 3x daily without fail.
* [ ] Sample collected from elbow depth away from jets.
* [ ] Pool closed immediately if FAC $< 1.0\text{ ppm}$ or pH $< 7.0 / > 8.0$.
* **Target Metric:** $100\%$ compliance with recommended water chemistry parameters.

2. **Dosing Application Guidelines:**
   * **Raising Free Chlorine:** Add Calcium Hypochlorite ($65\% - 70\%$) or Sodium Hypochlorite liquid. Pre-dissolve granules in a bucket of water before pouring slowly around pool perimeter.

   * **Lowering pH / Alkalinity:** Add Muriatic Acid (Hydrochloric Acid) poured slowly into deep end with pump running. **RULE:** Always add acid to water, NEVER add water to acid.

   * Perform heavy chemical dosing or chlorine shock treatment ($5.0 - 10.0\text{ ppm}$) strictly after pool closing (post-10:00 PM) to allow chemical dissipation before morning opening.
4. **Chemical Storage Facility Rules:**
   * Store pool chemicals in a dedicated, locked, well-ventilated, dry chemical shed.
   * Keep chlorine products physically separated from acid products by a minimum distance of $3\text{ meters}$ to prevent explosive gas reactions.

### 4. Localized Context
* **High Humidity Chemical Storage:** Northern Samar’s high humidity can turn powdered chlorine into clumpy mass and degrade potencies. Keep chemical drums tightly sealed with desiccants in raised plastic pallets off concrete floors.

### 5. Quality Assurance Checklist & Metrics
* [ ] PPE worn during all chemical measuring and mixing operations.
* [ ] "Acid to water" mixing rule strictly observed.
* [ ] Chlorine and acid stored in separate, dry, locked zones.
* **Target Metric:** $0$ chemical chemical handling safety incidents or chemical burn injuries.
