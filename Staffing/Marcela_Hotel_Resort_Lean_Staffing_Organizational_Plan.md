# Marcela's Hotel & Resort
## Lean Core Staffing & Organizational Structure Plan

**Property:** Marcela's Hotel & Resort  
**Location:** Gamay, Northern Samar, Philippines  
**Property Scope:** 30 Guest Rooms • Restaurant • Function Hall • Swimming Pool  
**Operating Model:** Lean core workforce + flexible on-call workforce

---

## 1. Executive Staffing Recommendation

Marcela's should **not** be staffed like a conventional full-service hotel with a large permanent workforce.

Because regular customer demand is expected to be concentrated on **weekends, holidays, high-occupancy periods, and events**, the recommended model is:

> **Lean regular core employees + a larger trained on-call pool.**

### Target Workforce

| Workforce Category | Recommended Headcount | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| **Core Regular Employees** | **8** | Maintain the property, handle reservations/administration, prepare for guests, and keep essential operations running |
| **On-Call / Flexible Pool** | **16–20** | Activate according to occupancy, weekends, events, holidays, and special requirements |
| **Contracted / Outsourced** | Variable | Security, IT/systems, specialized AV, and other specialist services |

### Operating Principle

**8 core employees keep the resort operational. The on-call team creates the capacity when business demand increases.**

This structure is designed to reduce fixed payroll while retaining the ability to operate a 30-room property and function-hall/event business during busy periods.

## 2. Core Regular Employee Structure

The recommended permanent/core workforce is **8 employees**.

These positions should be selected for **cross-functional capability** and should not be treated as narrowly defined single-task jobs.

| No. | Position | Regular | Primary Responsibility |

| 1 | **General Manager / Resort Manager** | 1 | Overall operations, management, sales coordination, events, staffing, service standards and decision-making |
| 2 | **Operations & Front Office Supervisor** | 1 | Front office, reservations, check-in/out, guest concerns, daily operating coordination and staff scheduling |
| 3 | **Guest Services / Reservations Officer** | 1 | Booking inquiries, reservations, guest communication, arrival/departure support and general guest services |
| 4 | **Housekeeping & Laundry Lead** | 1 | Room readiness, housekeeping standards, linen/laundry coordination, supplies and housekeeping scheduling |
| 5 | **Room Attendant / Utility Staff** | 1 | Guest rooms, public areas, laundry/utility assistance and general operational support |
| 6 | **Cook / Kitchen Lead** | 1 | Core food preparation, kitchen readiness, food safety and leadership of additional kitchen/event staff |
| 7 | **Maintenance & Pool Technician** | 1 | Facilities, utilities, preventive maintenance, pool, generator, electrical/plumbing coordination and property condition |
| 8 | **Admin / Finance / Purchasing Officer** | 1 | Bookkeeping, purchasing, records, payroll coordination, supplier transactions, permits and administrative support |
| **TOTAL** |  | **8** | **Lean year-round operating core** |

## 3. Why These 8 Positions Are the Core

The core team is deliberately limited to functions that must exist even when guest volume is low.

### Essential year-round functions

- Management and operating decisions
- Reservation and guest communication
- Basic front-office readiness
- Room and property readiness
- Laundry/linen control
- Basic food preparation
- Building and equipment maintenance
- Pool and utility monitoring
- Purchasing and inventory coordination
- Finance, documentation and administration

The property's SOP blueprint covers eight operating modules:

1. Front Office & Guest Services
2. Housekeeping & Laundry
3. Food & Beverage Operations
4. Function Hall & Events
5. Facilities, Engineering & Swimming Pool
6. Safety, Security & Typhoon Preparedness
7. Human Resources & Administration
8. Digital Systems, Records & Maintenance

The SOP blueprint identifies the property as a **30-room hotel and resort with a restaurant, function hall and swimming pool**. It includes procedures for reservations, guest service, room operations, F&B, catering, events, engineering, pool operations, security, emergencies, HR, purchasing, finance and digital systems.

## 5. Flexible / On-Call Workforce

Create a **pre-qualified and trained on-call roster of approximately 16–20 people**.

The objective is not to have 16–20 people working every weekend. The objective is to have enough qualified people available so management can scale staffing up or down according to demand.

### Recommended On-Call Roster

| Position | Recommended Pool |
|---|---:|
| Front Desk / Guest Service Relief | 2 |
| Room Attendants | 3–4 |
| Laundry / Utility Relief | 1 |
| Additional Cooks / Kitchen Helpers | 2–3 |
| Restaurant / Banquet Servers | 4 |
| Bartender / Barista | 1 |
| Event Setup / Banquet Crew | 2 |
| Pool Lifeguards | 2 |
| **TOTAL FLEXIBLE POOL** | **17–19** |

A roster of **16–20** is therefore a practical planning range.

## 6. Typical Staffing Activation

The resort should use a **demand-based activation model** rather than automatically scheduling the entire workforce.

### A. Low-Demand Weekday

**Core:** approximately 8 regular employees  
**On-call:** normally none, or only specific relief when needed

Primary priorities:

- Reservations and guest communication
- Property maintenance
- Room preparation for arrivals
- Laundry and linen
- Purchasing and administration
- Preventive maintenance
- Basic kitchen preparation
- Pool/facilities monitoring

### B. Normal Weekend With Hotel Guests

**Core:** 8 regular employees  
**On-call:** approximately **6–10**

Activate mainly:

- Front-office relief
- Additional room attendants
- Restaurant servers
- Kitchen helpers/cook
- Pool lifeguards

### C. Full / High-Occupancy Weekend

**Core:** 8 regular employees  
**On-call:** approximately **10–14**

Increase:

- Housekeeping
- Front desk coverage
- Restaurant service
- Kitchen production
- Pool coverage

### D. Wedding / Corporate / Government Event

**Core:** 8 regular employees  
**On-call:** approximately **12–18**

Activate:

- Banquet servers
- Additional cooks/kitchen helpers
- Event setup crew
- Guest reception/front-desk relief
- Pool/lifeguard coverage as required
- Additional maintenance/technical support as needed

The Function Hall & Events SOP module specifically covers event inquiry, proposals and quotations, contracts/deposits, planning, room setup, AV, registration, catering, suppliers, weddings, corporate/government events, event billing, post-event inspection and post-event feedback. These are demand-driven activities and support the use of a flexible event workforce.

## 7. Security Staffing Strategy

Security should preferably be **outsourced/contracted** rather than creating a large permanent security payroll at the beginning.

### Recommended arrangement

**Contract Security Agency / Licensed Security Personnel**

The resort should establish a security staffing requirement based on:

- Operating hours
- Guest occupancy
- Event size
- Night operations
- Pool operations
- Special events
- Emergency requirements

This allows security coverage to scale without making a large fixed payroll commitment.

The SOP blueprint includes extensive requirements covering fire safety, evacuation, first aid, drowning response, security incidents, CCTV, disaster command, typhoon preparedness, power outages, flooding, business continuity and incident reporting.

---

## 8. IT and Digital Systems Strategy

A full-time IT employee is **not recommended initially**.

Instead use:

> **External IT / Systems Specialist on monthly retainer + emergency call-out.**

The internal team should handle basic daily system use, while the external specialist supports:

- PMS / reservation system
- POS
- CCTV
- Wi-Fi/network
- Online booking platforms
- Digital payments
- Data backup/recovery
- Cybersecurity
- Website / Google Business Profile technical support
- User access and system troubleshooting

The SOP blueprint contains a dedicated Digital Systems module covering these areas.

## 10. Recommended Management Philosophy

Marcela's should operate as a:

> **Small hotel during low-demand periods + full-service resort/event venue during peak demand.**

The staffing system should therefore be built around **scalability**, not maximum permanent headcount.

### Core Principles

1. **Keep fixed payroll low.**
2. **Cross-train regular staff.**
3. **Maintain a reliable on-call talent pool.**
4. **Schedule additional labor only when revenue-generating demand exists.**
5. **Use specialized contractors when full-time utilization is too low.**
6. **Use events as the primary trigger for temporary labor expansion.**
7. **Review actual occupancy, event volume and labor cost monthly.**

| Regular Core | 8 | **~30–35%** |

The on-call employees are a **roster**, not a guarantee that all personnel will receive work every operating day or every weekend.

Management should monitor whether the lean staffing model is actually working.

Recommended monthly indicators:

| KPI | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Labor Cost as % of Revenue | Measures whether staffing remains financially sustainable |
| Labor Cost per Occupied Room | Measures hotel labor efficiency |
| On-Call Labor Cost | Measures flexible staffing utilization |
| Regular Payroll Cost | Controls fixed overhead |

| Revenue per Event | Helps determine event staffing economics |
| Guest Complaints / Service Failures | Ensures cost reduction does not damage service |
| Room Turnaround Time | Measures housekeeping capacity |

# Final Recommendation

For the business model described, the preferred starting structure is:

> ## **8 REGULAR CORE EMPLOYEES + 16–20 ON-CALL STAFF + OUTSOURCED SECURITY & IT**

This is the recommended **lean organizational model** for Marcela's Hotel & Resort.

The objective is to maintain a capable permanent skeleton while allowing the workforce to expand rapidly when the hotel has weekend occupancy, weddings, corporate/government functions, holidays, or other peak-demand periods.

The model should be reviewed after the first **6–12 months of actual operations** using occupancy, event volume, labor cost, guest complaints, service performance and revenue data. Positions should be converted from on-call to regular only when sustained demand demonstrates that the workload is sufficient to justify the additional fixed payroll.
