Module 05: Facilities, Engineering & Swimming Pool

Standard Operating Procedures for physical plant operations, preventive maintenance, electrical distribution, plumbing, HVAC, diesel generator backup, solar energy, and commercial swimming pool sanitation and safety.

Quick Jump to Procedure:

FM-01 Daily Facilities Inspection

Objective & Scope

Perform systematic daily morning walkthrough inspections across all physical structures, guest spaces, utility rooms, and public grounds to identify maintenance deficiencies, safety hazards, or equipment malfunctions before they impact guests.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Maintenance Supervisor: Conducts primary morning inspection walkthrough and assigns work tickets.
  • Duty Maintenance Technician: Assists in testing mechanical and electrical fixtures during the walkthrough.
  • General Manager: Performs random spot checks and reviews daily inspection logs.

Execution Procedure

  1. Initiate Morning Walkthrough (07:00 AM): Retrieve the Daily Facilities Walkthrough Checklist and master keys from the Engineering Office.
  2. Public Areas & Lobby Inspection:
    • Inspect all interior and exterior lighting fixtures for burnt-out bulbs or loose wiring.
    • Check lobby air conditioning operation, target setpoint at 22°C - 24°C.
    • Verify doors, glass panels, locks, and automatic closures operate smoothly.
  3. Walkway & Grounds Inspection:
    • Check pathway pavers, perimeter walls, and outdoor lamps for cracks, trip hazards, or weather damage.
    • Verify clear drainage pathways along garden perimeters.
  4. Utility & Plant Room Inspection:
    • Inspect main circuit breaker panels for excessive heat, buzzing sounds, or tripped breakers.
    • Record water supply pressure gauge readings (40 - 60 psi).
    • Inspect transfer pump and booster pump for leaks, abnormal vibration, or overheating.
    • Check backup diesel generator fuel gauge (≥ 80% full) and battery voltage meter (12V / 24V).
  5. Log & Action Assignment: Log all identified defects into the digital PMS Maintenance Module or Physical Engineering Logbook. Issue immediate work tickets for high-priority items.

Localized Context (Gamay, Northern Samar)

Gamay’s coastal atmosphere accelerates rust on outdoor metal lamps, door hinges, and screws. Inspect exterior metal fixtures daily for salt crusting and early corrosion. During the Samar rainy season, check garden drainage basins daily to prevent localized pooling near ground-floor guest rooms.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Walkthrough completed and logged prior to 08:30 AM daily.
  • Water supply pressure verified between 40 psi and 60 psi.
  • Generator fuel level verified at ≥ 80%.
  • Target Metric: 100% daily inspection completion rate; zero unaddressed high-priority hazards past 09:00 AM.
FM-02 Preventive Maintenance Program

Objective & Scope

Maintain a scheduled, proactive maintenance calendar for all guest rooms, mechanical equipment, HVAC systems, and building structures to extend asset life and minimize unexpected operational breakdowns.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Chief Engineer / Maintenance Supervisor: Manages the Master PM Schedule, orders spare parts, and oversees execution.
  • Maintenance Technicians: Executes scheduled PM tasks according to equipment checklists.
  • Front Office Manager: Coordinates room blocks for Room Preventive Maintenance (RPM) cycles.

Execution Procedure

  1. Master PM Calendar Maintenance: Establish annual, quarterly, monthly, and weekly preventive maintenance schedules covering:
    • Guest Rooms (RPM): Cycle through all 30 rooms (1 room per day, completing a full cycle every month).
    • HVAC Systems: Deep coil cleaning and filter washing quarterly.
    • Generators & Pumps: Engine oil, filter, and belt replacements every 250 run hours or semi-annually.
    • Pool Filtration: Backwashing sand filters weekly; deep pump strainer cleanouts bi-weekly.
  2. Room PM (RPM) Execution:
    • Block room with Front Office as "Out of Service - Maintenance" for 4 hours.
    • Test AC cooling performance, flush drain line, clean filters.
    • Inspect all plumbing fixtures, water heater tank, flush valve, and drain flow rate.
    • Inspect electrical outlets, light switches, TV/cable connection, and safe box operation.
    • Check furniture stability, silicon sealants around shower doors, and door lock battery voltage.
  3. Equipment PM Execution: Follow manufacturer service manuals for all heavy equipment (pumps, generator, kitchen freezers, water heaters).
  4. Sign-Off & Logging: Record completed PM tasks in the Master Asset Card and PMS PM Log.

Localized Context (Gamay, Northern Samar)

In Samar’s tropical climate, AC condensate lines accumulate mold rapidly. Flush all room AC drain lines with a mild bleach solution during monthly RPM to prevent indoor water leaks. Schedule comprehensive PM checks on all roof flashings, gutters, and generator systems prior to the peak typhoon season (September to December).

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • All 30 guest rooms undergo complete RPM at least once every 30 days.
  • Generator maintenance log updated after every service interval.
  • AC cooling temperature delta verified (≥ 8°C difference between intake and supply air).
  • Target Metric: ≥ 95% adherence to Master PM Schedule; < 2% emergency breakdown rate on PM-covered assets.
FM-03 Corrective Maintenance & Work Orders

Objective & Scope

Provide a fast, standardized workflow for reporting, prioritizing, assigning, executing, and closing corrective maintenance work orders across all departments.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Department Heads / Staff (Initiators): Spot defects and submit Work Orders via PMS or physical work order slips.
  • Maintenance Supervisor: Receives, prioritizes, and assigns work tickets to technicians.
  • Duty Maintenance Technician: Executes repairs and reports completion details.

Execution Procedure

  1. Work Order Creation: Upon discovering a defect (e.g., leaking faucet, dead light, noisy AC), the observing staff member creates a Work Order ticket specifying:
    • Location / Room Number
    • Specific Description of Problem
    • Date and Time Reported
    • Priority Level: Urgent (Life safety/Guest discomfort), High (Operational impairment), Normal (Cosmetic/Routine).
  2. Priority Triaging:
    • Urgent (Response < 15 mins): Power failure, active plumbing leak, AC failure in occupied room, door lock failure.
    • High (Response < 2 hours): Public restroom defect, kitchen equipment malfunction, pool light failure.
    • Normal (Response < 24 hours): Loose cabinet hinge, minor touch-up paint, garden fixture repair.
  3. Dispatch & Repair: Technician retrieves ticket, gathers necessary tools/parts from storage, and proceeds to location.
  4. In-Room Repair Protocol:
    • Knock 3 times, announcing "Maintenance Service."
    • If occupied, politely explain reason for visit and estimated duration.
    • Place drop cloth under work zone to protect resort carpet or tile floor.
    • Complete repair cleanly and sanitize work area post-repair.
  5. Testing & Sign-Off: Test repaired equipment in presence of requesting department lead or guest. Obtain requesting department signature on Work Order.

Localized Context (Gamay, Northern Samar)

If replacement parts are not in stock locally in Gamay and require procurement from Catarman or Tacloban (2 - 5 days lead time), immediately notify Front Office to keep the guest room out of order or offer room change.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Urgent work orders responded to within 15 minutes.
  • Repair site left completely clean and sanitized.
  • Requesting department signature obtained upon completion.
  • Target Metric: 90% of work orders resolved within target response time; 0 unaddressed guest-room tickets past 24 hours.
FM-04 Electrical System Inspection

Objective & Scope

Ensure safe, continuous electrical power distribution, prevent electrical fires, monitor voltage stability, and maintain all primary and secondary distribution panels across the property.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Licensed Electrician / Maintenance Tech: Performs electrical panel inspections, thermal testing, and load balancing.
  • Chief Engineer: Reviews electrical inspection logs and oversees high-voltage maintenance.

Execution Procedure

  1. Daily Main Breaker Panel Inspection:
    • Inspect Main Distribution Panel (MDP) and Sub-Distribution Panels (SDPs).
    • Measure incoming supply line voltage (220V ± 10%, 60 Hz).
    • Check panel door seals to prevent moisture, dust, or insect entry.
  2. Monthly Thermal Scan & Wire Tightening:
    • De-energize sub-panels during scheduled low-occupancy window using proper Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) safety protocol.
    • Check for loose terminal screws caused by thermal expansion; torque to manufacturer specification.
    • Use an infrared thermal scanner to detect overheating breakers or wiring busbars (temperature > 60°C indicates overload or loose connection).
  3. Grounding System Verification:
    • Inspect main ground rod connections quarterly.
    • Verify Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter (GFCI) outlets in bathrooms, kitchen wet zones, and swimming pool perimeter function correctly by pressing the "Test/Reset" button monthly.
  4. Emergency Lighting & Battery Bank Check: Test all emergency hallway exit signs and battery backup emergency lights monthly for a minimum 30-minute illumination run.

Localized Context (Gamay, Northern Samar)

The local electrical grid in Northern Samar experiences frequent voltage surges and brownouts. MANDATORY: Verify that main Automatic Voltage Regulators (AVRs) and surge protectors servicing sensitive hotel equipment (PMS server, elevators, kitchen refrigeration, AV gear) are operational daily.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Line voltage verified daily within 198V - 242V range.
  • All GFCI wet-zone outlets tested monthly.
  • Electrical panel thermal scan completed quarterly.
  • Target Metric: 0 electrical fire incidents; 100% functional GFCI outlet protection in wet zones.
FM-05 Plumbing Inspection & Repair

Objective & Scope

Maintain leak-free, clean, and efficient fresh water supply lines, water heaters, sanitary drainage, septic tanks, and kitchen grease traps throughout the resort.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Duty Plumber / Maintenance Tech: Conducts daily plumbing checks, unblocks lines, and services water heaters.
  • Executive Housekeeper: Reports room water leaks or slow drainage immediately to Engineering.

Execution Procedure

  1. Daily Water Pressure Check: Check manifold pressure gauges at booster pump output; maintain between 40 psi and 60 psi to ensure strong shower pressure across all 30 rooms.
  2. Weekly Guest Room Plumbing Audit:
    • Inspect under-sink angle valves, flex hoses, and p-traps for moisture or slow drips.
    • Test shower mixer valve, verify hot water delivery reaches 45°C - 50°C within 45 seconds.
    • Check toilet flush tank flapper valve and fill valve seal to eliminate silent water leaks into the bowl.
  3. Sanitary Sewer & Septic System Maintenance:
    • Inspect septic tank levels monthly. Schedule desludging via certified local waste contractor when solids reach 2/3 capacity.
    • Clean kitchen grease traps weekly: skim grease layer, clean filter basket, and dispose of grease according to environmental disposal guidelines.
  4. Drain Line Unblocking: Use drain snake or plunger for clogs. Avoid harsh chemical drain cleaners that damage PVC piping and destroy septic tank anaerobic bacterial cultures.

Localized Context (Gamay, Northern Samar)

Local deep well water sources in Northern Samar often contain elevated mineral content. Perform bi-monthly descaling of showerheads and water heater heating elements using vinegar or commercial descaling solution to prevent clogging.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Hot water temperature delivered at 45°C - 50°C within 45 seconds.
  • Kitchen grease traps cleaned and logged weekly.
  • Zero silent toilet flapper leaks active across 30 rooms.
  • Target Metric: Water loss from leaks maintained at < 3% of total utility consumption.
FM-06 Air-Conditioning Maintenance

Objective & Scope

Maximize cooling efficiency, maintain indoor air quality, optimize power consumption, and prevent water leakage from split-type and window-type air conditioning units across all rooms and public areas.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • HVAC Technician: Performs bi-weekly filter cleaning, quarterly chemical coil washing, and refrigerant pressure checks.
  • Maintenance Supervisor: Schedules preventive service cycles and monitors AC energy performance.

Execution Procedure

  1. Bi-Weekly Air Filter Cleaning:
    • Turn off AC power breaker. Remove air filters from evaporator unit.
    • Wash filters under clean tap water using soft brush; dry completely in shade before reinstalling.
  2. Quarterly Chemical Coil Cleaning:
    • Place protective waterproof jacket around indoor evaporator unit.
    • Apply biodegradable coil cleaning solution to aluminum fins; flush thoroughly with low-pressure washer.
    • Clear condensate drain tray and flush drain line using pressurized water or bleach mixture.
    • Wash outdoor condenser coil fins to remove dirt, salt crust, and debris.
  3. Refrigerant & Performance Audit:
    • Measure operating suction pressure (R410A: 120 - 140 psi; R32: 110 - 130 psi).
    • Measure air temperature differential (ΔT = T_return - T_supply). A properly operating unit must yield ΔT ≥ 8°C.
    • Check compressor running amperage against rated nameplate value.

Localized Context (Gamay, Northern Samar)

Coastal salt spray degrades aluminum condenser fins rapidly. Apply anti-corrosion blue-coat protective spray to all outdoor condenser coils semi-annually to extend coil life in Gamay's marine environment.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Air filters cleaned every 14 days.
  • Temperature differential ΔT ≥ 8°C verified post-service.
  • Condensate drain line flushed during every coil cleaning.
  • Target Metric: Zero in-room AC condensate leaks; 100% scheduled chemical wash completion rate.
FM-07 Water Supply & Pump System

Objective & Scope

Manage primary deep well systems, municipal water connections, filtration units, storage cisterns, and distribution pumps to ensure uninterrupted 24/7 supply of potable water across the property.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Duty Maintenance Technician: Monitors water levels, operates transfer pumps, and manages filtration backwash.
  • Chief Engineer: Oversees water quality testing and pump overhaul maintenance.

Execution Procedure

  1. Daily Water Level Monitoring: Inspect main underground cistern tank (50,000L capacity) and overhead day tank (10,000L capacity) at 06:00 AM, 12:00 PM, and 06:00 PM. Maintain combined volume at ≥ 75% capacity.
  2. Pump System Operation & Alternation:
    • Check primary and standby pressure booster pumps. Set control panel to automatic dual-pump alternator mode to ensure equal wear on pump motors.
    • Inspect pump mechanical seals for water leaks and verify motor operating temperature (< 70°C).
  3. Filtration & Chlorination Management:
    • Check inline multi-media sand filters and active carbon filters. Perform manual backwash when differential pressure exceeds 10 psi.
    • Test free chlorine level at the farthest guest room tap daily using DPD test kit. Target residual chlorine: 0.5 - 1.5 ppm.
    • Refill automatic chlorine dosing tank with sodium hypochlorite solution as needed.
  4. Monthly Water Bacteriological Test: Send water samples to an accredited DOH laboratory in Catarman monthly for E. coli and coliform analysis.

Localized Context (Gamay, Northern Samar)

Gamay municipal water pressure can fluctuate or drop without warning. Maintain automatic float switches on deep well transfer pumps to seamlessly refill cisterns whenever municipal pressure drops below 20 psi.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Cistern storage maintained at ≥ 75% capacity at all times.
  • Residual chlorine tested daily (0.5 - 1.5 ppm).
  • Monthly DOH-accredited water quality lab test passed (0 coliform count).
  • Target Metric: 100% water availability; zero water supply outages.
FM-08 Generator & Backup Power Procedure

Objective & Scope

Operate, test, and maintain the resort’s primary diesel generator set and Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS) to guarantee seamless emergency power restoration within 15 seconds of an electrical grid outage.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Duty Maintenance Tech / Generator Operator: Performs daily checks, fuel management, and manual changeovers if required.
  • Chief Engineer: Supervises load testing, ATS servicing, and fuel supplier agreements.

Execution Procedure

  1. Daily Generator Readiness Inspection:
    • Check diesel fuel level in main base tank; must remain ≥ 80% full (minimum 24 hours continuous full-load run capacity).
    • Check engine oil level, coolant radiator level, and drive belt tension.
    • Verify starter battery trickler charger voltage (13.5V - 14.2V).
    • Ensure ATS control panel switch is set to "AUTO" mode.
  2. Automatic Outage Sequence Execution:
    • Upon grid power failure, ATS senses line loss, signals generator engine start within 3 seconds, and transfers load within 10 - 15 seconds.
    • Duty Tech immediately verifies generator voltage (220V), frequency (60 Hz), and engine water temperature (80°C - 90°C).
  3. Manual Override Procedure (In Case of ATS Failure):
    • Switch ATS selector switch from "AUTO" to "MANUAL".
    • Press "START" button on generator control panel; allow engine warm-up for 30 seconds.
    • Manually throw ATS main breaker switch to "GENERATOR" position.
  4. Weekly No-Load / Monthly Load Testing: Run generator under actual resort load for 30 minutes on the 1st and 15th day of every month to keep engine lubricated and purge moist air from alternator windings.

Localized Context (Gamay, Northern Samar)

Northern Samar experiences scheduled and unscheduled power blackouts from NORSAMELCO. Keep a minimum reserve stock of 500L of clean diesel fuel on-site in approved double-walled storage tanks to handle prolonged regional power outages.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Automatic transfer completed within 15 seconds of grid failure.
  • Fuel level maintained at ≥ 80% daily.
  • Monthly load test executed and recorded in Generator Logbook.
  • Target Metric: 100% backup power reliability during grid outages.
FM-09 Solar Power & Energy System Monitoring

Objective & Scope

Monitor, clean, and maintain the resort's solar photovoltaic (PV) array and hybrid inverter system to maximize renewable energy output and reduce utility power expenses.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Solar Systems Technician / Maintenance Staff: Performs solar panel cleaning, inverter readings, and cable checks.
  • Chief Engineer: Tracks daily power production analytics (kWh) against resort utility benchmarks.

Execution Procedure

  1. Daily System Inspection (08:00 AM & 04:00 PM):
    • Inspect solar inverter display panel. Verify status reads "NORMAL / GRID-TIED".
    • Record daily generation metric (kWh generated), peak output power (kW), and fault error logs.
    • Check battery bank (if equipped) state of charge (SOC) and cell temperatures (< 35°C).
  2. Bi-Weekly Solar Panel Cleaning:
    • Clean panels during early morning (06:00 AM - 07:30 AM) when glass surfaces are cool to prevent thermal shock breakage.
    • Spray panels with low-pressure fresh water; wipe clean using soft microfiber squeegees to remove salt haze, dust, bird droppings, and leaves.
    • Never use harsh chemical detergents or abrasive brushes on anti-reflective glass coatings.
  3. Monthly Cable & Mounting Frame Audit:
    • Inspect aluminum mounting rails and stainless steel clamps for corrosion or wind looseness.
    • Check DC junction boxes and MC4 connectors for weather seals and heat damage.

Localized Context (Gamay, Northern Samar)

Sea breeze coats solar panels in a thin salt layer that can reduce energy yield by 15% - 20%. Increase cleaning frequency to weekly during dry, windy months.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Panels cleaned every 14 days (or weekly during dry wind conditions).
  • Inverter generation metrics recorded daily.
  • Zero physical thermal damage on MC4 connectors and wiring harness.
  • Target Metric: Solar system performing at ≥ 90% of rated design generation profile.

FM-11 Building & Structural Inspection

Objective & Scope

Conduct periodic structural integrity assessments of concrete foundations, timber supports, roof trusses, exterior walls, staircases, and resort decking to prevent structural deterioration.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Chief Engineer / Structural Inspector: Conducts quarterly structural audits and manages masonry/carpentry repairs.
  • General Manager: Authorizes major structural remediation projects.

Execution Procedure

  1. Quarterly Structural Audit Execution:
    • Concrete Elements: Check exterior pillars, beams, and foundation walls for hairline cracks, spalling concrete, or exposed rebar.
    • Timber Structures: Inspect wooden posts, roof trusses, gazebos, and pool decks for rot, soft spots, warping, or termite infestation.
    • Walls & Paint: Check exterior paint for peeling, water penetration stains, or efflorescence (salt leeching).
  2. Defect Remediation:
    • Concrete Spalling: Chip away loose concrete, treat exposed rebar with anti-corrosion primer, apply high-strength epoxy repair mortar.
    • Timber Rot: Replace rotted wood members with pressure-treated hardwood; seal with weather-resistant exterior wood varnish.
  3. Annual Paint Refresh Schedule: Schedule annual exterior wall repainting using high-grade elastomeric waterproof paint.

Localized Context (Gamay, Northern Samar)

Chloride ions from sea spray penetrate porous concrete, causing internal rebar to rust, expand, and spall concrete off beams. Inspect all ocean-facing pillars quarterly for rust bleed marks.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Quarterly structural audit executed and documented with photos.
  • Exposed rebar treated with anti-corrosion coating immediately upon detection.
  • Exterior paint integrity maintained without visible peeling or mold staining.
  • Target Metric: Zero structural safety incidents; 100% remediation of detected cracks within 30 days.

Inspect roof coverings, flashing seals, downspouts, and rainwater gutter channels monthly and before major weather events to prevent water ingress and roof structure damage.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Maintenance Technicians: Performs roof climbing inspections, gutter clearing, and sealant repairs.

Execution Procedure

  4. Pre-Typhoon Roof Audit: Execute an immediate mandatory roof check upon issuance of PAGASA Typhoon Signal #1.

Localized Context (Gamay, Northern Samar)

Gamay’s lush foliage drops large palm fronds and heavy organic matter onto roofs. Clogged downspouts during Samar downpours cause gutters to overflow into room ceiling cavities within minutes.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Target Metric: Zero active roof leaks during heavy monsoon downpours.

FM-16 Contractor Access & Maintenance Work

FM-18 Pool Water Testing

Objective & Scope

Measure, analyze, and record pool water chemistry parameters three times daily to guarantee guest safety, prevent waterborne illnesses, and protect pool filtration equipment from corrosion or scaling.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Pool Attendant / Certified Pool Operator: Collects water samples, performs DPD/Phenol Red chemical tests, and logs metrics.
  • Maintenance Supervisor: Audits test logs and calculates corrective chemical dosing schedules.

Execution Procedure

  1. Testing Schedule: Perform water testing 3 times daily:
    • Test 1: 05:30 AM (Pre-opening)
    • Test 2: 12:00 PM (Peak sun / High bather load)
    • Test 3: 05:00 PM (Late afternoon)
  2. Sample Collection Method: Take water sample at elbow depth (18 inches / 45 cm below surface) away from return jets and skimmer openings.
  3. Parameter Ranges & Standard Testing Protocol:
    • Free Available Chlorine (FAC): Ideal range 1.0 - 3.0 ppm (Use DPD #1 tablet/reagent).
    • pH Level: Ideal range 7.2 - 7.8 (Use Phenol Red reagent).
    • Total Alkalinity (TA): Tested weekly; Ideal range 80 - 120 ppm.
    • Cyanuric Acid (CYA): Tested monthly; Ideal range 30 - 50 ppm.
  4. Action Thresholds: If Free Chlorine drops < 1.0 ppm or pH strays outside 7.0 - 8.0, close pool immediately to guests until chemical balance is restored.
  5. Logbook Entry: Record all test values, ambient weather, bather count, and added chemical dosages into the Master Pool Water Logbook.

Localized Context (Gamay, Northern Samar)

Intense tropical sun in Northern Samar rapidly degrades free chlorine via UV rays (requiring proper stabilizer/CYA levels). Heavy tropical rains dilute chlorine and lower pH drastically; test water immediately post-downpours.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Water tested and logged 3x daily without fail.
  • Sample collected from elbow depth away from jets.
  • Pool closed immediately if FAC < 1.0 ppm or pH < 7.0 / > 8.0.
  • Target Metric: 100% compliance with recommended water chemistry parameters.
FM-19 Pool Chemical Management

Objective & Scope

Govern the safe handling, precise dosing calculation, storage, and application of pool treatment chemicals including chlorine, pH adjusters, algaecides, and flocculants.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Certified Pool Operator / Attendant: Measures and applies chemicals safely.
  • Maintenance Supervisor: Manages chemical inventory and hazardous material compliance.

Execution Procedure

  1. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) Mandate: ALWAYS wear safety goggles, heavy chemical gloves, rubber apron, and dust mask/respirator when handling pool chemicals.
  2. Dosing Application Guidelines:
    • Raising Free Chlorine: Add Calcium Hypochlorite (65% - 70%) or Sodium Hypochlorite liquid. Pre-dissolve granules in a bucket of water before pouring slowly around pool perimeter.
    • Raising pH: Add Soda Ash (Sodium Carbonate) pre-dissolved in water.
    • Lowering pH / Alkalinity: Add Muriatic Acid (Hydrochloric Acid) poured slowly into deep end with pump running. RULE: Always add acid to water, NEVER add water to acid.
  3. Timing of Chemical Application: Perform heavy chemical dosing or chlorine shock treatment (5.0 - 10.0 ppm) strictly after pool closing (post-10:00 PM) to allow chemical dissipation before morning opening.
  4. Chemical Storage Facility Rules: Store pool chemicals in a dedicated, locked, well-ventilated, dry chemical shed. Keep chlorine products physically separated from acid products by a minimum distance of 3 meters to prevent explosive gas reactions.

Localized Context (Gamay, Northern Samar)

Northern Samar’s high humidity can turn powdered chlorine into clumpy mass and degrade potencies. Keep chemical drums tightly sealed with desiccants in raised plastic pallets off concrete floors.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • PPE worn during all chemical measuring and mixing operations.
  • "Acid to water" mixing rule strictly observed.
  • Chlorine and acid stored in separate, dry, locked zones.
  • Target Metric: 0 chemical handling safety incidents or chemical burn injuries.

FM-21 Pool Filtration & Pump Operation

Objective & Scope

Operate recirculation pumps, backwash media sand filters, monitor pressure differentials, and ensure complete water turnover every 6 hours.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Maintenance Tech / Certified Pool Operator: Manages pump room operations, backwashing, and mechanical valve adjustments.
  • Chief Engineer: Oversees pump motor overhauls and filter media replacement cycles.

Execution Procedure

  1. Daily Pump Room Audit:
    • Inspect circulation pump running sound, check mechanical shaft seal for water drips.
    • Read filter pressure gauge. Baseline clean filter pressure: 10 - 15 psi.
  2. Sand Filter Backwash Procedure (Required when gauge reads 8 - 10 psi above baseline):
    • Turn OFF main pool pump power switch.
    • Turn multiport valve handle to "BACKWASH" position.
    • Open backwash sight glass drain line valve.
    • Turn ON pump; run for 2 - 3 minutes until water in sight glass turns crystal clear.
    • Turn OFF pump. Turn multiport valve handle to "RINSE".
    • Turn ON pump; run rinse cycle for 1 minute to settle sand bed.
    • Turn OFF pump. Reset multiport valve handle to "FILTER". Open main return valves and turn ON pump.
  3. Turnover Rate Standard: Run circulation pumps for a minimum of 12 - 16 hours daily to achieve a minimum of 3 complete water turnover cycles per day.

Localized Context (Gamay, Northern Samar)

When grid power cuts and the generator starts, pump control panels must feature a 3-minute time-delay relay to prevent electrical current surges from damaging pump motor windings during power transfer.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Filter backwashed whenever pressure rises 8 - 10 psi above baseline.
  • Multiport valve handle turned ONLY when pump is powered OFF.
  • Minimum 3 complete water turnovers executed daily.
  • Target Metric: 100% operational pump recirculation; zero pump burnout failures.

FM-25 Pool Accident & Drowning Response

Objective & Scope

Provide an immediate, coordinated emergency response plan for aquatic distress, near-drowning, or major physical injury occurring in or around the swimming pool.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • First Responder (Pool Attendant / Security / Staff): Executes rescue, extracts victim, and initiates CPR.
  • Front Office Manager / Duty Manager: Calls local emergency medical services (EMS), coordinates ambulance entry, and alerts General Manager.
  • General Manager: Manages family communication, legal documentation, and incident reporting.

Execution Procedure

  1. RESCUE INITIATION (Seconds Count):
    • Spot swimmer in distress. Sound emergency whistle (3 long blasts) to alert nearby staff.
    • Extend Shepherd’s Hook or throw Ring Buoy if victim is conscious.
    • If victim is unconscious or submerged, enter water immediately, lift victim’s head above water, and extract onto pool deck maintaining neck/spine stabilization.
  2. ASSESSMENT & CPR (0 – 2 Minutes):
    • Place victim flat on back. Check responsiveness and breathing.
    • If not breathing, assign specific staff member: "YOU! Call Front Office for Ambulance and bring the First Aid Kit and AED immediately!"
    • Begin CPR immediately: 30 chest compressions followed by 2 rescue breaths (using CPR pocket mask). Continue cycle without interruption until victim revives or EMS arrives.
  3. EMERGENCY MEDICAL DISPATCH:
    • Front Office immediately dials Gamay Municipal Health Office / Emergency Rescue or Catarman Provincial Hospital Ambulance.
    • Security clears front driveway for fast ambulance entry.
  4. POST-INCIDENT PROTOCOL:
    • Close pool area immediately; clear all guests from deck.
    • Preserve scene; take high-resolution photographs of pool water clarity, chemical levels, and equipment.
    • Complete formal Resort Medical Incident Report within 2 hours.

Localized Context (Gamay, Northern Samar)

Gamay Health Center provides basic stabilization, but advanced trauma care requires transit to Catarman (1.5 - 2 hours by road). Continuous on-site CPR capabilities are vital during transport preparation.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Emergency whistle sounded and staff dispatched immediately upon incident discovery.
  • CPR initiated within 60 seconds of victim extraction.
  • Front Office dispatched ambulance without delay.
  • Target Metric: Rapid rescue execution (< 30 seconds water access time); 100% staff adherence to CPR protocol.
