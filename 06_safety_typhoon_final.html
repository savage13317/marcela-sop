Module 06: Safety, Security & Typhoon Preparedness

Emergency protocols, fire safety, security operations, typhoon response, and disaster preparedness for Marcela's Resort Hotel in Gamay, Northern Samar.

Quick Jump to Procedure:

SOP-SS-01 Hotel Emergency Response System

Objective & Scope

To establish an Incident Command System (ICS) and emergency classification framework that directs resort personnel during critical threats, natural disasters, fires, medical crises, or security incidents to safeguard life, property, and business continuity.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Incident Commander (IC): General Manager or highest-ranking Duty Manager on shift.
  • Safety & Security Officer: Manages immediate tactical response and physical perimeter control.
  • Front Office Supervisor: Coordinates guest communications, headcounts, and external authority contacts.
  • Chief Engineer / Maintenance Lead: Manages utility isolation, emergency power, and structural integrity checks.

Execution Procedure

  1. Emergency Level Classification:
    • Level 1 (Minor / Localized): Isolated event manageable by shift staff (e.g., minor pipe leak, localized small fire quickly extinguished, minor guest injury).
    • Level 2 (Moderate / Escalated): Requires multi-departmental coordination and potential local municipal assistance (e.g., localized structural damage, grid power blackout exceeding 4 hours, severe medical emergency, active intruder).
    • Level 3 (Major / Critical Emergency): Severe threat to life or property requiring total resort response and full activation of municipal disaster agencies (e.g., Signal #3+ Typhoon, uncontrolled fire, structural failure, drowning).
  2. Incident Command System (ICS) Activation:
    • Upon escalation to Level 2 or Level 3, the IC declares ICS activation via two-way radio Channel 1.
    • Command Center established at the Front Desk Office (Primary) or Executive Office (Secondary).
  3. Role Assignment & Execution:
    • IC delegates operational roles: Security (Perimeter/Access), Engineering (Utilities/Safety), Front Office (Guest Accounting), F&B (Emergency Rations).
  4. Log & Timeline Keeping:
    • IC designates a log keeper to document all decisions, authority calls, timeline events, and casualty/injury reports in real time.

Localized Context

Medical and fire response times in Gamay, Northern Samar may extend past standard urban benchmarks due to geographical distance and road conditions. The hotel must operate as a self-sufficient emergency unit for at least 2 to 4 hours before major provincial assistance arrives.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • ICS Command Center fully operable within 10 minutes of emergency escalation.
  • Radio Channel 1 clear and dedicated exclusively to Emergency Command.
  • Master emergency log initiated and maintained continuously during the event.
  • Target Metric: 100% staff role clarity during drills; zero response delays due to command confusion.
SOP-SS-02 Fire Prevention & Fire Safety

Objective & Scope

To maintain rigid fire prevention standards, inspect fire suppression hardware, enforce safe LPG and electrical handling, and conduct regular maintenance to eliminate fire risks across all 30 rooms, restaurant kitchen, function hall, and maintenance facilities.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Safety Officer / Chief Engineer: Oversees fire safety compliance, extinguisher servicing, and alarm testing.
  • Head Chef: Enforces kitchen fire safety, grease trap cleaning, and LPG shutoff routines.
  • Housekeeping Supervisor: Ensures emergency hallways, fire doors, and stairwells remain unobstructed.

Execution Procedure

  1. Monthly Fire Extinguisher Audit:
    • Inspect all ABC Dry Chemical, CO2, and Wet Chemical (K-Class) fire extinguishers across the property.
    • Check pressure gauge (needle must remain strictly in the green zone).
    • Verify safety pin and tamper seal are intact. Sign and date inspection tag.
  2. Kitchen Fire Safety Protocols:
    • Kitchen wet chemical suppression system above commercial stoves inspected semi-annually.
    • Exhaust hood filters cleaned weekly to prevent heavy grease accumulation.
    • LPG main supply line valves closed tightly every night post-kitchen closure.
  3. Electrical Fire Hazard Audits:
    • Daily check of utility panels for loose wiring, burning odors, or tripping breakers.
    • Ban high-wattage guest appliances (e.g., portable electric stoves) not provided by the resort.
  4. Quarterly Fire Alarm & Smoke Detector Testing:
    • Test optical smoke detectors in guest rooms, hallways, and function hall quarterly using aerosol smoke testers.

Localized Context

Salt air in Gamay corrodes fire extinguisher nozzles and steel brackets. Coat metal brackets with anti-corrosion grease quarterly and replace rusted safety pins immediately.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • 100% of fire extinguishers inspected, pressurized, and signed monthly.
  • Kitchen exhaust hoods deep-cleaned weekly without fail.
  • LPG safety shut-off valve verified closed every night at F&B close.
  • Target Metric: Zero fire incidents; 100% pass rate on Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP Gamay) inspections.
SOP-SS-03 Fire Alarm Response

Objective & Scope

To define the rapid, step-by-step verification and response procedure upon activation of the main Fire Alarm Control Panel (FACP) to prevent panic, identify false alarms, or initiate immediate fire suppression and evacuation.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Front Desk Agent: Monitors Fire Alarm Control Panel (FACP) and dispatches floor response.
  • Duty Security Officer / Duty Technician: Proceeds immediately to indicated zone for physical verification.
  • Duty Manager: Takes overall command of response and decides on building evacuation.

Execution Procedure

  1. FACP Signal Triggered:
    • Alarm horn sounds at Front Desk panel indicating specific zone (e.g., "Zone 3 - 2nd Floor Guest Corridor").
    • Front Desk Agent notes time and zone, silences panel buzzer locally, but DOES NOT reset the system.
  2. Immediate Physical Verification (Target Time: < 2 mins):
    • Front Desk dispatches Duty Security and Duty Tech via radio: "Security, verify Zone 3 immediately."
    • Security runner proceeds to zone with primary ABC fire extinguisher and master access card.
  3. Condition Assessment & Reporting:
    • Case A: FALSE ALARM (Dust, insect, steam): Security reports "Code Green - False Alarm" back to Front Desk. Tech resets panel. Front Desk logs cause.
    • Case B: REAL FIRE / UNCONTROLLED SMOKE: Security shouts warning, attempts initial suppression using extinguisher if small (< 1 sq. meter). If fire spreads, security reports "CODE RED Zone 3" via radio.
  4. Code Red Alarm Activation:
    • Front Desk Agent immediately hits the General Building Evacuation Siren on FACP.
    • Front Desk Agent dials BFP Gamay Emergency Line immediately.

Localized Context

High humidity or sea mist can occasionally trigger sensitive smoke detectors. Staff must execute physical verification rapidly (< 120 seconds) before triggering full resort panic, while treating every alarm as a real fire until proven otherwise.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Zone verification completed on-site within 2 minutes of panel alarm.
  • Master FACP never reset prior to physical site confirmation.
  • BFP Gamay emergency speed-dial functional at Front Desk.
  • Target Metric: 100% compliance with the 2-minute verification rule; zero unverified panel resets.
SOP-SS-04 Fire Evacuation Procedure

Objective & Scope

To execute a swift, orderly, and complete evacuation of all hotel guests, staff, and visitors during an active fire or major life-safety threat using the R.A.C.E. protocol.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Incident Commander (GM/Duty Manager): Directs total building evacuation and liaises with BFP Gamay.
  • Floor Evacuation Wardens (Housekeeping / Security): Sweeps hallways, guest rooms, and public areas.
  • Front Desk Agent: Retrieves Master Guest In-House List, key cards, and emergency mobile phone.
  • Assembly Area Coordinator: Conducts guest headcount at designated assembly zone.

Execution Procedure

  1. Evacuation Declaration: General Evacuation Siren sounded continuous pattern. Emergency Voice Announcement made over public address system: "Attention all guests and staff: Please proceed calmly via the nearest exit to the main assembly area on the front lawn."
  2. Execute R.A.C.E. Protocol:
    • R - Rescue: Guide guests in immediate danger to safety without putting staff lives at unreasonable risk.
    • A - Alarm: Ensure FACP general alarm is active and BFP Gamay called (911 / Local BFP).
    • C - Contain: Close all guest room doors and corridor fire doors behind evacuating persons to starve fire of oxygen.
    • E - Evacuate: Direct everyone to exterior exit routes. NEVER USE ELEVATORS.
  3. Floor Wardens Room Sweep:
    • Wardens knock loudly, unlock every room on assigned corridor: "Fire Alarm! Evacuate immediately!"
    • Mark checked room doors with a designated evacuation tag or leave door wide open once verified empty.
  4. Assembly Area Headcount:
    • Direct all evacuees to Assembly Area A (Main Garden Lawn near Entry Gate).
    • Front Desk Agent cross-checks evacuees against active PMS In-House Guest List and Staff Shift Roster.
    • Report missing persons immediately to BFP Fire Ground Commander.

Localized Context

Fires frequently trip main circuit breakers or damage wiring. Backup battery emergency exit lights automatically illuminate exit routes for 30–60 minutes. Floor wardens must carry heavy-duty LED flashlights attached to their duty belts at all times.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • PMS active guest list printed or accessed digitally within 3 minutes of evacuation signal.
  • Complete building floor sweep completed in < 5 minutes.
  • Headcount accounting completed at assembly area within 10 minutes.
  • Target Metric: 100% guest and staff safety accounting; zero structural entrapment.
SOP-SS-05 Emergency Exit & Assembly Area

Objective & Scope

To ensure all structural emergency exits, escape corridors, stairwells, and primary outdoor assembly zones remain illuminated, clearly signed, unobstructed, and ready for immediate deployment 24/7.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Safety Officer: Inspects exit hardware, emergency push bars, and signage illumination.
  • Public Area Houseman: Ensures no furniture, linen carts, or boxes block exit pathways.
  • Security Guard on Duty: Verifies exit doors are unlocked from the inside (panic hardware operational).

Execution Procedure

  1. Daily Pathway & Exit Door Inspections:
    • Walk all emergency stairwells, corridors, and exterior exit gates daily at 07:00 AM.
    • Verify panic push bars on secondary emergency exit doors open smoothly outwards with < 15 lbs of pressure.
    • Ensure zero padlocks or chains are placed on emergency exit doors during operational hours.
  2. Exit Signage & Emergency Lighting Standards:
    • Photoluminescent or powered LED "EXIT / LABASAN" signs must remain lit continuously.
    • Test battery backup exit light units monthly by pressing the test button; lights must remain illuminated for full 30-second test.
  3. Assembly Zone Maintenance:
    • Assembly Area A (Primary): Main Garden Lawn near Front Entrance Gate.
    • Assembly Area B (Secondary / Tsunami Evacuation): High-ground concrete structure / Function Hall 2nd Floor (if coastal storm surge or tsunami alert coincides).
    • Keep assembly area signboards clean, unobstructed, and well-lit at night.

Localized Context

Gamay is a coastal Samar municipality. If a fire or evacuation coincides with a strong offshore earthquake or marine surge alert, Assembly Area B (designated high ground / elevated structure) must be utilized instead of low-lying beach lawns.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • 100% of emergency exit doors unlocked from inside and opening freely.
  • Zero obstructions (linen carts, trash bins, maintenance ladders) in fire exits.
  • Emergency lights tested monthly and functional.
  • Target Metric: Zero exit pathway safety violations during internal or external audits.
SOP-SS-06 First Aid & Medical Emergency

Objective & Scope

To provide immediate, competent basic first aid assessment, life support stabilization, and medical referral for guests or staff experiencing sudden illness, trauma, or injury on resort grounds.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Certified First Responder (Shift Staff / Security): Administers immediate basic first aid / CPR.
  • Front Office Agent / Duty Manager: Coordinates emergency ambulance calls and guest medical transport.
  • Safety Officer: Maintains first aid kits, AED equipment, and medical supply inventory.

Execution Procedure

  1. Initial Assessment (Scene Safety & Patient Status):
    • Ensure scene is safe to enter. Approach victim, check responsiveness (A-V-P-U: Alert, Verbal, Pain, Unresponsive).
    • Call for back-up staff and retrieve First Aid Kit + Automated External Defibrillator (AED) from Front Desk.
  2. First Aid Administration:
    • Minor Cut / Abrasion: Clean wound with sterile saline solution, apply povidone-iodine, cover with sterile gauze and adhesive bandage.
    • Heat Exhaustion / Fainting: Move patient to cool shaded area, loosen tight clothing, elevate feet, offer oral rehydration salts (ORS) in small sips if conscious.
    • Severe Trauma / Fractures: Immobilize injured limb, apply direct pressure to heavy bleeding using thick sterile pads. DO NOT move patient with suspected spinal injury unless immediate life threat exists.
  3. Medical Emergency Transport Escalation:
    • If patient exhibits severe symptoms (chest pain, breathing difficulty, deep unconsciousness, severe bleeding, stroke signs):
      • Contact Gamay Rural Health Unit (RHU) / Municipal Health Office (MHO) immediately.
      • Prepare resort emergency vehicle (or local municipal ambulance) for transport to Catarman Provincial Hospital for advanced trauma/cardiac care.
  4. Medical Incident Documentation: Record patient name, room number, incident details, vital signs, first aid given, and transport destination in the Resort Medical Logbook.

Localized Context

Gamay RHU provides basic stabilization. Emergency transport to the primary tertiary hospital in Catarman, Northern Samar takes approximately 1.5 to 2 hours by ambulance. Early triage and prompt transport calls are vital for serious conditions.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • First Aid kits stationed at Front Desk, Kitchen, Engineering, and Pool Deck fully stocked.
  • Expiration dates on medications, antiseptics, and burn gels audited monthly.
  • Shift team includes at least one active BLS/CPR certified staff member per shift.
  • Target Metric: Emergency medical response initiated within < 3 minutes of notification.
SOP-SS-07 Guest Accident / Injury Reporting

Objective & Scope

To govern the sensitive management, thorough investigation, factual documentation, and empathetic care following any slip, fall, guest accident, or injury occurring anywhere on resort property.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Duty Manager: Takes immediate charge of guest care, empathy communication, and formal report generation.
  • Security Officer: Photographs scene, secures evidence, and gathers witness statements.
  • General Manager: Manages long-term guest relations, insurance claims, and legal considerations.

Execution Procedure

  1. Immediate Medical Care First: Prioritize guest health and medical assistance immediately. Never debate fault or liability while a guest is in pain.
  2. Secure & Photograph the Incident Scene:
    • Security Officer inspects exact location immediately post-incident.
    • Take clear photos of floor conditions, lighting, signage (e.g., "Wet Floor" sign presence), footwear, and physical obstacles.
    • Check CCTV footage covering the area; save raw video clip covering 30 minutes prior to and post-accident.
  3. Witness Statement Collection: Collect written, signed statements from staff or third-party witnesses detailing observed events objectively.
  4. Factual Incident Report Completion: Duty Manager completes Formal Guest Incident Report within 4 hours, specifying:
    • Guest full name, room number, contact info.
    • Exact date, time, weather conditions, lighting condition.
    • Factual description of incident (avoid subjective admissions like "It was our fault" or "We forgot to mop").
    • Medical assistance offered and guest's response / refusal signed.
  5. Follow-Up & Empathy Care: Duty Manager or GM makes courtesy call or room visit post-treatment to check guest recovery status.

Localized Context

Sudden tropical rain squalls in Gamay make smooth exterior tiles slippery. Staff must document whether rain or pool water contributed to the slip and verify caution cones were posted.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Medical attention prioritized before any report writing.
  • CCTV footage archived within 2 hours of incident.
  • Formal Incident Report submitted to GM within 4 hours.
  • Target Metric: 100% of guest accidents documented completely; zero lost evidence or unarchived CCTV clips.
SOP-SS-08 Drowning / Pool Emergency

Objective & Scope

To define the critical water rescue, victim extraction, cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), automated external defibrillation (AED), and emergency notification workflow during a pool drowning or near-drowning emergency.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Primary Rescuer (Pool Attendant / Security / Staff): Executes immediate water rescue and CPR.
  • Secondary Rescuer: Calls emergency assistance, retrieves AED and First Aid Kit.
  • Front Office Agent: Dispatches ambulance and clears driveway entry.

Execution Procedure

  1. RESCUE INITIATION (Target: < 20 seconds to water entry):
    • Spot swimmer in distress. Sound whistle loudly (3 long blasts) to alert staff.
    • Enter water, support victim's head and neck, extract victim safely onto pool deck.
  2. CPR & AED SEQUENCE (Target: < 60 seconds post-extraction):
    • Lay victim flat on back on pool deck. Check breathing and pulse for max 10 seconds.
    • If non-breathing / no pulse:
      • Assign staff member: "You! Call Front Desk for Ambulance and bring the AED!"
      • Begin CPR immediately: Give 5 initial rescue breaths, followed by 30 chest compressions to 2 rescue breaths at a rate of 100 - 120 compressions/min.
      • Apply AED pads as soon as unit arrives (dry victim's chest quickly before applying pads). Follow AED voice prompts strictly.
  3. AMBULANCE DISPATCH & SCENE MANAGEMENT:
    • Front Desk dials Gamay RHU / Emergency Ambulance service.
    • Clear all swimming guests from pool and close pool gates immediately.
  4. POST-INCIDENT PROTOCOL: Keep pool closed until water clarity, chemical balance, and police/management incident reports are fully cleared.

Localized Context

If Gamay municipal ambulance is delayed, immediately utilize the resort's dedicated emergency van driven by a trained resort driver to meet the incoming provincial ambulance en route to Catarman.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Water rescue initiated within 20 seconds of distress observation.
  • CPR commenced within 60 seconds of extraction.
  • AED pads applied correctly following voice guidance.
  • Target Metric: 100% staff execution speed on emergency resuscitation protocols.
SOP-SS-09 Missing Guest / Person Procedure

Objective & Scope

To provide a systematic protocol for receiving reports, searching resort grounds, reviewing security systems, and coordinating with local law enforcement when a registered guest or minor child is reported missing.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Front Office Supervisor: Receives missing report, verifies registration, logs details.
  • Security Lead: Directs physical search sweep of resort grounds and CCTV review.
  • General Manager: Escalates case to Philippine National Police (PNP Gamay) and family liaisons.

Execution Procedure

  1. Information Gathering & Verification:
    • Receive report from family or companion. Note missing person's name, age, physical description, clothing, room number, medical conditions, last seen time, and phone number.
    • Attempt phone call / messaging to missing guest's cellular phone immediately.
  2. Internal Grounds Search (Phase 1 — 0 to 30 Mins):
    • Security dispatches all available staff to perform organized search sweep of grounds: Guest room, pool area, garden perimeter, public restrooms, restaurant, beach line, maintenance sheds.
    • Review CCTV cameras covering main gates, beach path, and guest floor corridors from the last seen timestamp.
  3. Off-Property Escalation (Phase 2 — 30 to 60 Mins):
    • Check local Gamay commercial area, nearby eateries, and coastal pathways.
    • If missing person is a MINOR CHILD or VULNERABLE ADULT, escalate to PNP Gamay and Barangay Captain immediately without waiting 24 hours.
  4. Police & Family Coordination: Provide PNP Gamay with registered ID copy, recent photograph (if available from family), CCTV clips, and search timeline.

Localized Context

Because Gamay borders coastal waters, search sweeps must immediately prioritize checking the beach shoreline and pool waters during the first 10 minutes of a missing person report.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Internal resort physical search completed within 30 minutes.
  • CCTV log reviewed back to last seen timestamp within 20 minutes.
  • Immediate police notification for missing minors (< 30 minutes).
  • Target Metric: Zero unaccounted missing guest incidents; 100% compliance with search timelines.
SOP-SS-10 Suspicious Person / Security Incident

Objective & Scope

To maintain perimeter security, identify unauthorized or suspicious individuals loitering on resort property, de-escalate potential conflicts, and safely remove trespassers.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Duty Security Officer: Intercepts suspicious persons, conducts verbal challenges, enforces access controls.
  • Front Desk Agent: Assists security via radio monitoring and police escalation calls.
  • Duty Manager: Manages formal trespass notices and police turn-overs.

Execution Procedure

  1. Identification & Observation: Identify individuals lacking guest wristbands/key cards, loitering near guest corridors, parking areas, or restricted service zones.
  2. Professional Approach & Verification (Contact Procedure):
    • Approach with hands open, maintaining 2-meter non-confrontational safety distance.
    • Greet politely and challenge: "Good day, sir/ma'am! Welcome to Marcela's. May I ask if you are a registered staying guest, dining at our restaurant, or visiting someone?"
  3. De-Escalation & Directional Handling:
    • If Valid Guest/Diner: Direct them courteously to Front Desk or Restaurant.
    • If Unregistered Trespasser: State resort policy firmly: "Sir/Ma'am, this is private resort property reserved for registered guests. I must kindly ask you to step outside the main entry gate." Escort them calmly to perimeter gate.
  4. Hostile / Non-Compliant Escalation:
    • If individual refuses to leave, acts aggressively, or displays weapons:
      • DO NOT engage in physical combat unless defending life.
      • Maintain distance, alert Duty Manager, hit silent hold-up alarm / call PNP Gamay Station Emergency Line.
      • Lock resort lobby glass doors to secure guests inside.

Localized Context

Rural provincial culture values warmth and openness. Security officers must balance vigilant access control with polite provincial hospitality so genuine local visitors or walk-in diners feel welcomed rather than intimidated.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • 100% of non-registered visitors intercepted and greeted prior to entering guest room corridors.
  • PNP Gamay emergency contact programmed into security mobile handsets.
  • De-escalation techniques prioritized over physical force.
  • Target Metric: 0 unauthorized intrusions into guest room corridors.
SOP-SS-11 Theft / Property Loss Investigation

Objective & Scope

To govern the objective, professional investigation of reported guest or resort property loss/theft, ensuring proper evidence gathering, guest safe electronic audits, CCTV review, and police reporting.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Security Supervisor: Leads factual investigation, safe audits, and CCTV cross-checks.
  • Executive Housekeeper: Conducts staff interview for room attendant schedules.
  • General Manager: Handles guest relationship management and formal PNP crime reporting.

Execution Procedure

  1. Receiving Loss Report:
    • Receive guest report empathetically in a private room (Executive Office). Avoid making immediate promises or defensive denials.
    • Record exact item description, estimated value, serial numbers, last seen location, and time window.
  2. Room Safe Audit & Access Verification:
    • If loss reported from in-room digital safe, execute handheld audit trail extraction using master safe programmer device.
    • Print safe log displaying precise timestamps of every keycard swipe, pin code unlock, or master override key usage.
    • Retrieve PMS electronic door lock audit trail showing all staff and guest entries during the period.
  3. CCTV Audit & Staff Interview:
    • Review corridor CCTV footage covering room entrance for suspicious entries or staff activity during time window.
    • Interview assigned room attendants and maintenance staff privately, taking written statements.
  4. Police Referral & Formal Incident Report:
    • If theft is confirmed or guest wishes to file formal criminal charges, assist guest in visiting PNP Gamay Police Station.
    • Provide PNP investigating officer with printed safe/lock audit trails and relevant CCTV footage.

Localized Context

Theft allegations in small provincial communities require discreet handling to protect both guest dignity and local staff reputations. Investigations must rely strictly on objective digital audit evidence (safe logs, lock audits, CCTV).

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Safe and door lock electronic audit pulled within 2 hours of report.
  • Corridor CCTV footage archived covering full time window.
  • Confidentiality maintained throughout investigation.
  • Target Metric: 100% factual resolution rate based on objective digital evidence.

To operate, monitor, maintain, and restrict access to the resort’s closed-circuit television (CCTV) system, ensuring maximum property security while respecting guest privacy rights and data protection laws.

  2. System Operation & Recording Standards:

Localized Context

Samar grid power fluctuations can damage DVR hard drives and power supplies. Ensure all CCTV NVR units and camera power buses operate behind online Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS) battery systems with surge suppression.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • 30-day continuous archive recording verified active daily.
  • Zero camera blind spots on main gates, pool deck, and cashier zones.
  • CCTV log updated for every video review or export instance.

SOP-SS-14 Disaster Incident Command

Objective & Scope

To structure leadership, delegation, communication, and operational decision-making during severe natural disasters (typhoons, earthquakes, major floods) through a formal Command Center layout.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Incident Commander (IC): General Manager / Resident Manager.
  • Operations Lead: Security & Housekeeping Supervisor.
  • Logistics & Supply Lead: F&B & Materials Controller.
  • Planning & Comm Lead: Front Office Supervisor.

Execution Procedure

  1. Declaration of Disaster Command: Activated when PAGASA issues Typhoon Signal #2 or higher, or following a major seismic event (> Magnitude 6.0).
  2. Command Center Setup: Establish main command desk in the Executive Office / Front Desk backroom equipped with:
    • Battery-powered VHF multi-channel radios.
    • Satellite phone / cellular internet backup terminals.

    • Heavy-duty flashlights and emergency battery packs.
  3. Briefings & Operations Rhythm: Hold 10-minute briefing every 4 hours with all department heads to assess:
    • Guest safety and headcount status.
    • Water, food, and diesel fuel consumption rates.
    • Structural damage or utility supply cuts.
  4. Authority Liaison: IC serves as sole official contact point for Gamay MDRRMO, Provincial Governor's Office, and media inquiries.

Localized Context

Severe typhoons can isolate Gamay from regional assistance for 48 to 72 hours due to fallen trees or landslides along Samar highways. The Disaster Command must manage resources independently during this isolation window.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Command Center operational within 15 minutes of disaster signal declaration.
  • Whiteboard tracking operational status updated every 4 hours.
  • Radio communications established across all department leads.
  • Target Metric: Uninterrupted leadership continuity throughout disaster lifecycle.
SOP-SS-15 Typhoon Monitoring & Alert Levels

Objective & Scope

To track PAGASA (Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration) weather bulletins, establish standard resort alert triggers, and execute progressive safety responses based on Tropical Cyclone Wind Signals (TCWS #1 to #5).

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Duty Front Desk Agent: Tracks PAGASA weather bulletins every 3 hours during tropical weather events.
  • Incident Commander / GM: Issues resort alert level changes based on official PAGASA data.

Execution Procedure

  1. Weather Tracking Routine: Monitor official PAGASA severe weather bulletins continuously via website, radio, and Gamay MDRRMO bulletins during tropical depression alerts.
  2. Resort Progressive Action Matrix:
    TCWS #1 (39-61 km/h) ──► STAGE BLUE (Heightened Awareness)
    TCWS #2 (62-88 km/h) ──► STAGE YELLOW (Active Property Preparation)
    TCWS #3 (89-117 km/h) ──► STAGE ORANGE (Full Lockdown & Guest In-Room Safety)
    TCWS #4/5 (>118 km/h) ──► STAGE RED (Maximum Emergency Defense & Survival Mode)
  3. Stage Specific Operational Triggers:
    • STAGE BLUE (TCWS #1): Inspect gutters, test backup generator, verify fuel levels (≥ 500L diesel reserve), brief staff.
    • STAGE YELLOW (TCWS #2): Secure loose outdoor pool/garden furniture, tape large window panes if required, issue initial guest weather advisory letter to all 30 rooms.
    • STAGE ORANGE (TCWS #3): Ban swimming pool and outdoor walking, enforce guest curfew, deploy sandbags to ground-floor thresholds, initiate emergency generator run checks.
    • STAGE RED (TCWS #4/5): Complete lockdown of all glass doors, assemble non-secure room guests into reinforced Function Hall concrete zone, monitor structural integrity continuously.

Localized Context

Gamay lies directly along the Eastern Visayas typhoon belt. Weather conditions can deteriorate rapidly from Signal #1 to Signal #3 within 12 hours. Staff must execute preparation tasks early without waiting for local rainfall to begin.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • PAGASA bulletins logged every 3 hours during active weather systems.
  • Resort Stage transitions declared promptly upon official PAGASA signal issuance.
  • Guest weather advisories printed and distributed at Stage Yellow.
  • Target Metric: 100% completion of stage preparations prior to storm landfall.
SOP-SS-16 Pre-Typhoon Property Preparation

Objective & Scope

To detail the physical hardening, outdoor clearing, window protection, drainage clearing, and structural securing of all resort buildings prior to typhoon landfall.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Chief Engineer: Coordinates structural securing, roof tie-downs, shutter installation, and drainage clearing.
  • Housekeeping & Grounds Team: Clears outdoor furniture, secures linen, and prepares emergency sandbags.

Execution Procedure

  1. Grounds & Outdoor Furniture Clearing (Stage Yellow - TCWS #2):
    • Remove all pool loungers, umbrellas, garden tables, plastic chairs, hanging signage, and trash bins from outdoor spaces.
    • Store outdoor furniture securely inside the ground-floor storage room or Function Hall backroom.
  2. Roof, Gutters & Drainage Hardening:
    • Clear leaves and debris from all roof gutters and storm drain basins to prevent roof overflow.
    • Inspect tin roof sheets; apply emergency roofing screws and tie-down guy wires where necessary.
    • Trim hazardous tree branches overhanging guest room roofs or power lines.
  3. Window & Glass Door Protection:
    • Close and lock all storm shutters across ocean-facing glass doors and windows.
    • Apply heavy cross-pattern tape across non-shuttered glass panes to minimize flying glass shrapnel if shattered.
  4. Sandbag Deployment: Place pre-filled sandbags against lobby entrance doors, restaurant glass doors, and ground-floor guest room thresholds to block driven rainwater ingress.

Localized Context

Typhoon winds hitting coastal Gamay carry heavy salt water and high velocity. Unsecured lightweight objects (e.g., plastic trash cans, aluminum chairs) turn into dangerous projectiles that smash glass doors.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • All outdoor furniture cleared from grounds 6 hours prior to expected landfall.
  • Storm drains and roof gutters 100% clear of leaves.
  • Sandbags deployed at all exterior doors.
  • Target Metric: Zero avoidable property damage caused by windblown loose resort objects.

Objective & Scope

SOP-SS-18 Power Outage / Brownout Procedure

Objective & Scope

To manage main grid power loss, ensure automatic standby generator transfer within 15 seconds, monitor fuel consumption, protect sensitive electronic gear, and maintain guest room power continuously.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Duty Maintenance Technician: Monitors ATS transfer, generator load, engine oil, and fuel levels.
  • Front Desk Agent: Reassures guests, monitors elevator status (if applicable), logs outage duration.

Execution Procedure

  1. Automatic Grid Failure Response (0 - 15 Seconds):
    • Main grid power fails (NORSAMELCO outage).
    • Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS) senses voltage loss, triggers diesel generator start, and transfers resort load within 15 seconds.
    • Emergency lighting in corridors and public areas illuminates instantly.
  2. Technician Generator Verification (1 - 5 Minutes):
    • Technician checks generator panel: Voltage (220V ± 5%), Frequency (60 Hz), Running Amperage, Oil Pressure, and Engine Temperature (80°C - 90°C).
    • Verify Automatic Voltage Regulator (AVR) stabilizes load.
  3. Extended Outage Fuel Management (Outages > 4 Hours):
    • Check fuel gauge every 2 hours. Log fuel consumption (Liters/Hour).
    • Refill main base tank from reserve diesel drums when fuel drops to 50% capacity.
  4. Grid Power Restoration Procedure:
    • Grid power restored and stabilized for minimum 5 continuous minutes.
    • ATS automatically transfers load back to grid. Generator executes 3-minute cool-down cycle before shutting down.

Localized Context

Unscheduled brownouts occur frequently in Northern Samar. The resort's ATS and generator system must remain on standby 24/7. Critical servers, PMS, and CCTV must run behind UPS battery backups so data is never lost during the 15-second generator transfer window.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • ATS transfer completed in < 15 seconds of grid failure.
  • Generator parameters (220V / 60 Hz) verified stable post-transfer.
  • Minimum 500L diesel reserve maintained in storage at all times.
  • Target Metric: 100% emergency power transition success rate; zero server crashes.
SOP-SS-19 Flooding / Heavy Rain Response

Objective & Scope

To deploy submersible dewatering pumps, sandbags, and drainage controls to prevent water intrusion into ground-floor guest rooms, lobby, and electrical rooms during torrential monsoonal downpours.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Chief Engineer / Maintenance Lead: Operates drainage pumps, monitors water levels, isolates electrical breakers if water rises.
  • Housekeeping Team: Deploys sandbags, wet vacuums, and water barriers at building entrances.

Execution Procedure

  1. Drainage System Activation & Check: Upon onset of continuous heavy monsoonal rain, inspect perimeter storm drainage channels. Clear leaves from inlet grates immediately.
  2. Submersible Pump Deployment:
    • Activate primary sump pumps in basement / lower utility pits.
    • Position portable 2-inch diesel/electric submersible pumps at low-lying garden zones threatened by water pooling.
  3. Physical Water Barriers (Sandbagging): Stack pre-filled sandbags in staggered overlapping wall formation across lobby doors and ground-floor room patio doors if external water levels reach 5 cm.
  4. Electrical Safety Isolation Threshold: If water level rises within 10 cm of electrical wall outlets or sub-panel boards in any zone, IMMEDIATELY SHUT OFF the circuit breaker for that specific floor/zone to prevent fatal electrocution hazards.

Localized Context

Northern Samar experiences intense localized rainfall during the Northeast Monsoon (Amihan). Water accumulation can happen rapidly. Sump pumps must be tested weekly during monsoon months (October to February).

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Submersible pumps tested and operational weekly during monsoon season.
  • Sandbags stocked and pre-filled in dry storage (50 bags minimum).
  • Circuit breakers marked clearly for fast zone isolation during flood threats.
  • Target Metric: Zero floodwater entry into guest rooms or electrical plant rooms.
SOP-SS-20 Post-Typhoon Damage Assessment

Objective & Scope

To conduct a systematic, safe physical sweep of all structures, electrical lines, water systems, roof trusses, and guest spaces immediately following typhoon passage before clearing guests to exit rooms.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • Assessment Team Lead (Chief Engineer): Inspects structural, electrical, and mechanical assets.
  • Safety Officer: Clears perimeter zones for downed power wires, glass, and fallen trees.
  • General Manager: Compiles damage assessment report for insurance and owners.

Execution Procedure

  1. Initial Exterior Safety Sweep (Phase 1 — Staff Only):
    • Once wind drops completely and PAGASA lifts Signal #3/#2, Assessment Team exits to sweep property.
    • Hazard Check: Look for downed high-voltage power lines, unstable tree limbs, dangling roof sheets, broken glass, or contaminated water.
    • Mark hazardous zones with red "DANGER - DO NOT ENTER" warning tape.
  2. Structural & Utility Assessment (Phase 2):
    • Inspect all 30 guest room roofs, ceiling water stains, window seal integrity.
    • Inspect water cisterns for flood contamination; test pool water chemistry.
    • Check diesel fuel reserves and utility power lines.
  3. Photo & Video Documentation: Take high-resolution photographs and videos of all physical damages before clearing debris for insurance claims.
  4. Resort All-Clear Declaration: Once property safety is verified 100% by Assessment Team, GM issues formal "Resort All-Clear" announcement allowing guests to leave rooms safely.

Localized Context

Fallen power poles or wires along Gamay roads may remain energized by private secondary generators even during grid outages. Treat every fallen line as live and dangerous until verified clear by Engineering.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Full safety sweep executed prior to allowing guests out of rooms.
  • All structural damages photographed and logged with GPS/time stamps.
  • Hazard zones cordoned off with red warning tape.
  • Target Metric: Zero post-typhoon injuries during cleanup and recovery phase.

Execution Procedure

    • If PMS / internet systems fail, transition Front Desk to Manual Registration Ledgers and physical cash receipts.
    • Maintain physical room rack board for guest status tracking.

SOP-SS-24 Incident Report & Post-Incident Review

Objective & Scope

To conduct mandatory post-incident debriefings, evaluate operational emergency performance, execute root-cause analysis, complete formal legal documentation, and update SOP protocols within 48 hours of any major emergency.

Responsible Staff Roles

  • General Manager: Convenes post-incident review meeting, approves final Master Incident Report.
  • Safety Officer: Leads root-cause analysis and compiles department evidence/logs.
  • Department Heads: Submit shift logs, timelines, and corrective action recommendations.

Execution Procedure

  1. Post-Incident Debrief Meeting (Within 24 Hours):
    • Convene Incident Command team and key shift staff for formal operational debriefing.
    • Review event timeline step-by-step against written SOP procedures.
    • Identify what worked successfully, what failed, and where communication broken down.
  2. Root Cause Analysis (RCA): Execute "5-Why Analysis" on any equipment failures, procedural delays, safety breaches, or injury occurrences.
  3. Master Incident Report Generation (Within 48 Hours): Safety Officer compiles comprehensive report containing:
    • Executive Summary & Detailed Event Timeline.
    • Casualty / Injury Log and Medical Reports.
    • Property Damage Quantification & Cost Estimates.
    • Police / BFP / MDRRMO official reports attached.
    • Photographs, safe/lock audit prints, and CCTV evidence logs.
  4. SOP & Operational Manual Revision: If debrief reveals gaps in current SOPs, update relevant SOP modules within 7 days, re-issue version control numbers, and conduct mandatory staff retraining.

Localized Context

Every major typhoon or infrastructure failure in Samar provides valuable local lessons. Documenting exact failure points (e.g., specific roof leaks, drainage bottlenecks) ensures the property builds stronger resilience prior to the next storm season.

QA Checklist & Key Metrics

  • Post-incident debrief meeting held within 24 hours of event closure.
  • Master Incident Report finalized and submitted to owners/insurance within 48 hours.
  • Corrective action items assigned with clear deadlines and responsible owners.
  • Target Metric: 100% closure of assigned post-incident corrective action items within 14 days.
